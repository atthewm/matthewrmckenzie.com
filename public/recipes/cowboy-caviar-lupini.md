A cold marinated bean salad rebuilt around lupini beans, which carry roughly twice the protein per calorie of black or kidney beans. Soy-free, heavy on citrus, and layered with heat. Serves 8 as a side and keeps 5 days.

## Read first: lupini preparation
**Dry lupini beans are not safe to eat without multi-day debittering.** They contain quinolizidine alkaloids (lupanine, sparteine) that are neurotoxic, and there are documented poisoning cases from improperly prepared home batches.

**Heat does not remove the alkaloids; repeated water changes do.** Do not slow cook them. A slow cooker holds the beans in their own alkaloid-laden liquid and concentrates it.

1. Soak dry lupini in cold water 24 hours. They roughly double in size.
2. Boil 45 minutes. Drain and discard the water.
3. Submerge in fresh cold water, refrigerated. Change the water twice daily.
4. Starting day 5, taste one bean daily. Done when there is zero bitterness.
5. If it is bitter, it is not ready. That is the entire test.

Buy "sweet" (low-alkaloid) lupini if available for a much shorter debitter. Most dry lupini sold is the bitter type. Expect 7 to 10 days total, nearly all passive.

Lupini never soften like other beans. Permanently firm is correct, and it is exactly why they hold up in this salad for five days. Yield from dry runs roughly 2.3x by weight, but varies.

## Ingredients

### Beans and vegetables
- 800 g lupini beans, debittered and drained (from ~350 g dry)
- 200 g artichoke hearts, canned in water, drained and rinsed
- 400 g English cucumber, seeded and diced
- 200 g broccoli slaw
- 200 g red bell pepper, diced
- 200 g Roma tomatoes, seeded and diced
- 150 g radishes, diced
- 80 g red onion, finely diced
- 75 g fire-roasted corn, frozen
- 60 g jalapeños, minced, seeds in
- 20 g serrano pepper, minced, seeds in
- 25 g chipotle in adobo, minced, plus 1 tsp sauce
- 30 g cilantro, chopped

### Citrus-chile dressing
- 100 ml fresh lime juice (~4 limes)
- Zest from 3 limes
- 1 orange, zested and juiced (30 ml juice)
- 60 ml red wine vinegar
- 1 tbsp avocado oil
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1½ tsp ground cumin
- 1½ tsp smoked paprika
- 1½ tsp chili powder
- ½ tsp cayenne
- ½ tsp kosher salt

## Instructions
1. **Check the lupini.** Taste one. Any bitterness at all means back into fresh water for more days. Drain and pat dry.
2. **Salt and drain the cucumber.** Toss with a pinch of salt in a colander and let sit 20 minutes while you prep. This is what keeps the batch from going soupy by day two.
3. **Prep the rest.** Rinse and drain the artichokes. Thaw the corn and pat dry. Dice the bell pepper, tomatoes, radishes, and onion. Seed the tomatoes; the gel is the other thing that waters this down. Mince the chiles, seeds in. Chop the cilantro.
4. **Build the dressing.** Zest before juicing. Whisk everything together until emulsified. It is deliberately acid-heavy, since it has 2.7 kg of salad to season.
5. **Combine.** Pat the cucumber dry, fold everything together, pour the dressing over, and toss thoroughly.
6. **Chill 2 hours minimum.** Longer than a standard bean salad; lupini are dense and slow to absorb.

## Notes
- **Why lupini.** It beats every other common legume on protein per calorie, ahead of kidney, black, and chickpea by a wide margin.
- **Why artichokes.** Lupini is a protein bean, not a fiber bean (2.8 g fiber per 100 g vs 8.7 g for black beans). Dropping the other beans left a fiber gap. Artichoke hearts fill it at nearly twice the fiber per calorie of black beans while lowering overall calorie density. They also hold texture in acid for a week.
- **Home prep vs canned.** Canned lupini run 200 to 800 mg sodium per 100 g even after draining. Home-prepped and unbrined is near zero.
- **Calorie density.** This build lands at 0.61 cal/g, down from 1.06 for a conventional cowboy caviar. The levers, in order of impact: cut the corn, drop the second and third legumes, and bulk with high-water vegetables (cucumber 0.15, radish 0.16, broccoli slaw 0.25 cal/g). Butternut squash is a poor choice here: 0.45 cal/g raw, concentrating to ~0.75 roasted, and the sweetness fights the acid.
- **Heat builds.** Capsaicin migrates into the dressing over 48 hours. Build to slightly under your target.
- **Storage.** Lupini, radish, and artichoke hold through day 5. Cucumber softens by day 3, so hold half back and fold in fresh if you want the full week.
- **Avocado.** Add per portion at plating only, never into the batch. ¼ avocado adds 80 kcal / 7 g fat / 3 g fiber.
