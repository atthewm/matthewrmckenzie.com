Chipotle adobo built from dried chiles instead of canned, using MSG to carry savoriness at a fraction of the sodium. Keeps 3 weeks refrigerated, or freeze in an ice cube tray at 1 tbsp per cube.

## Ingredients
- 30 g dried chipotle chiles, about 6 (meco preferred)
- 30 g tomato paste, no salt added
- 60 ml apple cider vinegar
- 40 g white onion, rough chopped
- 3 garlic cloves
- 1 tsp Mexican oregano
- ½ tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp honey
- 2 g MSG (~½ tsp)
- 0.5 g kosher salt

Salt is specified by weight deliberately. Diamond Crystal runs ~2.8 g/tsp and Morton ~4.8 g/tsp, a 70% difference by volume. Weigh it.

## Instructions
1. **Toast.** Stem the chiles and shake out most seeds; keep a few for heat. Toast in a dry skillet over medium, 30 seconds per side, until fragrant and pliable. Do not let them darken or smoke; scorched chile turns bitter and there is no fixing it.
2. **Soak.** Cover with hot water for 20 minutes, until fully soft. Reserve the soaking liquid; it is carrying smoke.
3. **Blend.** Combine the chiles, tomato paste, vinegar, onion, garlic, oregano, cumin, honey, and ~60 ml of the soaking liquid. Blend smooth, adding liquid to reach a loose ketchup consistency.
4. **Simmer 10 minutes.** Gently, stirring, until it darkens slightly and the raw vinegar edge cooks off. This step is what turns chile puree into adobo.
5. **Season off heat.** Stir in the MSG and salt after the simmer, then taste. Adding them at the end means you are seasoning the finished concentration, not guessing at it.
6. **Cool and jar.**

## Notes

### Why MSG here
MSG is ~12% sodium by weight; table salt is ~39%. Gram for gram it delivers roughly one-third the sodium, and because it hits umami receptors rather than salt receptors, it adds a dimension salt cannot reach on its own. To get this much savory depth with salt alone you would need roughly 2.5 to 3 g of salt (~1,000 to 1,180 mg sodium). The MSG-plus-salt blend here lands at ~470 mg for the entire batch.

MSG needs a little sodium chloride alongside it; on its own it reads flat and slightly metallic. The 0.5 g of salt is doing structural work, not seasoning work.

MSG is FDA GRAS. The "Chinese restaurant syndrome" claims from the late 1960s have not held up under controlled double-blind testing.

| Version | Sodium per tbsp |
|---|---:|
| Canned chipotle in adobo | ~100 mg |
| This recipe | ~36 mg |
| Zero-salt version (omit MSG and salt) | ~2 mg |

Dried chipotle chiles carry roughly 30 mg sodium per 100 g. Essentially all the sodium in canned product comes from the packing brine and added sauce salt, not the chile.

### Morita vs. meco
Morita is the reddish-purple chipotle, smoked briefly, fruitier and softer. Meco is tan-gray, smoked far longer, and carries roughly double the smoke intensity per gram. Meco is harder to find and worth the hunt; it is the reason this tastes like adobo rather than chile puree.

### Using it: three tiers
Built to be dialed rather than committed to. Each tier is independent.
- **Tier 1, infusion:** Toast one chile, crack it, steep in warm vinegar or citrus for 30 minutes, and strain it out. Smoke and heat carried into a dressing with zero added mass and zero sodium.
- **Tier 2, targeted soak:** Toss a discrete ingredient in 2 tbsp adobo for 30 minutes before folding it into a dish. Fire-roasted corn is the best vehicle: it already has char notes to build on, stays a concentrated pocket rather than diffusing, and its sweetness plays against the smoke. Red onion is second best and distributes more evenly.
- **Tier 3, per-portion finish:** Keep the jar in the fridge and spoon 1 tsp over the plated portion. The batch stays neutral and the decision moves to plating time. 4 kcal, ~12 mg sodium.

### More tips
- **Freeze in cubes.** 1 tbsp per ice cube tray well. Three weeks in the fridge, indefinitely frozen.
- **The base is the asset.** Made without MSG or salt this is a genuinely zero-sodium ingredient (~2 mg/tbsp), useful anywhere the sodium budget is tight. Seasoning at the end preserves that option.
- **Scale the heat with seeds, not chiles.** More chiles shift the flavor toward bitter and vegetal; retained seeds raise heat cleanly.
- **Pairs with the Cowboy Caviar (Lupini Build).** Swapping 60 ml of this for the 25 g of canned chipotle, plus cutting the salad's salt, drops that recipe from ~215 mg to ~123 mg sodium per serving while roughly tripling the chipotle presence.
