A house favorite. One pound of lamb stew meat oven-braised at 300°F in a stainless skillet under a parchment cartouche, with sweet potato, artichoke hearts, and pomegranate molasses. No lid, no Dutch oven, no pot required.

## Ingredients
- 454 g lamb stew meat, lean cut, patted dry
- 2 tsp no-salt seasoning
- ½ tsp salt
- 2 tsp avocado oil
- 1 medium white onion, sliced thin
- 2 tbsp pickle juice
- 1½ tsp umami seasoning blend (Simply Organic)
- 1 tsp garlic
- 1 tsp mustard
- 1 tbsp pomegranate molasses, plus ½ tsp to finish (substitute: 1 tbsp honey)
- 200 g fresh pico de gallo
- 3 cups chicken bone broth (2 cups to start, ~1 cup in top-ups)
- 300 g cubed sweet potato
- 150 g frozen artichoke hearts
- ½ medium lemon, zested and juiced
- 3 green onions, sliced
- 10 g Parmesan, grated
- ¼ tsp black garlic finishing salt

## Instructions
1. **Preheat and cut the cartouche.** Oven to 300°F. Cut a parchment circle slightly wider than the pan, fold into a cone, snip a dime-sized hole at the tip, and unfold.
2. **Season.** Pat the lamb bone dry. Toss with the no-salt seasoning and salt.
3. **Sear.** Heat the oil over medium-high until shimmering. Sear the lamb in two batches, single layer, ~4 minutes per side. Stainless releases the meat once the crust forms, so don't move it early. Set aside.
4. **Onion.** Drop to medium. Cook the onion 4 minutes until translucent, scraping up the browned bits.
5. **Deglaze and build.** Add the pickle juice and scrape the pan clean. Stir in the umami blend, garlic, mustard, and 1 tbsp pomegranate molasses. Add the pico and 2 cups broth. Return the lamb and resting juices; the liquid should sit halfway up the meat.
6. **Cartouche on.** Bring to a bare simmer and lay the cartouche directly on the liquid surface (not on the rim; the edges ride up the pan sides). Move to the oven.
7. **Braise.** At 300°F, check the liquid at 45 minutes and top up. Fork-check at 70 minutes. The target texture yields easily but holds its shape. Pulled-pork shred means overcooked and stringy.
8. **Add sweet potato and liquid.** At 70 minutes, add ~¾ to 1 cup more broth (sweet potato displaces volume and absorbs liquid), stir, nestle the potato in, and re-seat the cartouche. Braise 30 minutes more.
9. **Artichoke hearts.** Move to the stovetop over medium-low, discard the cartouche, and stir in the artichokes uncovered for 8 minutes. Frozen vegetables dump water, and cooking uncovered lets the sauce reduce back.
10. **Finish off heat.** Add the juice and zest of ½ lemon plus the reserved ½ tsp pomegranate molasses. Top with green onion, Parmesan, and finishing salt.

## Notes
- **Searing alone won't tenderize this cut.** Collagen needs sustained moist heat around 160 to 180°F to convert to gelatin.
- **Lean-cut timing:** fork-check at 70 minutes, not 90. Lean lamb hits tender earlier and turns stringy past the window. Less rendering fat also means the braise depends more on broth level, so top up attentively.
- **Cartouche technique:** a parchment circle cut wider than the pan with a dime-sized vent hole (½ to ¾ inch). Lay it in contact with the liquid, not resting on the rim. The hole vents steam so the parchment stays seated; err small.
- **Artichoke hearts beat broccoli slaw here.** They push the dish fully Levantine, which is where the pomegranate, lemon, and lamb were already pointing.
- **Pomegranate molasses over honey.** Its tartness cuts the lamb's richness and deepens the sauce color. The bright top notes cook off, so hold back ½ tsp for the finish. The lemon is cut to half to compensate for the added acid.
- **Worcestershire** (1 to 2 tsp) is a legitimate mid-braise addition, adding anchovy and tamarind depth that disappears into the sauce. Sweet sauces (Bachan's, BBQ) fight the profile.
- **Sodium:** a 430 g portion is ~45% of a 2,300 mg day. The drivers are the ½ tsp rub salt (~1,150 mg per batch) and the finishing salt, not the broth (~120 mg per cup). Cut or halve the rub salt to bring it down.
- Skip milk; tomato plus lemon will curdle it. Parmesan goes on top, not stirred in.
- Parsley or mint would land better than green onion with the artichokes.

### Making it a bigger meal
- **More protein:** add about 100 g cooked chicken breast to the bowl for roughly 31 g more protein and ~165 calories, without changing the flavor profile.
- **More volume:** stir in 150 to 200 g roasted vegetables. Roasted cauliflower works especially well with the Levantine profile, adding ~50 to 70 calories and 3 to 5 g fiber.
- Both together is the full-meal version.
