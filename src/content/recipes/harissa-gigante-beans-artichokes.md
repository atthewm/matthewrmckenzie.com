Dry gigante beans braised low and slow in chicken bone broth with harissa, bloomed spices, and onions, with artichoke hearts added near the end and a squeeze of lemon to finish. Made in a 4 to 5 quart saucepan.

## Ingredients
- 340 g dry gigante beans (1 bag, SIMPLi)
- 946 ml chicken sipping bone broth (1 full box)
- Water, to 3 L total liquid
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- ½ purple onion, diced
- 3 bay leaves
- 1 tbsp (15 g) spicy harissa (Mina)
- 1 tsp custom EBS blend
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 340 g frozen artichoke hearts (1 full bag)
- ½ lemon, juiced, added off heat
- Avocado oil, a thin film for the sauté

No garlic salt; the harissa and EBS blend carry it.

## Instructions
1. Dice both onions. Over medium heat with a thin film of avocado oil, add the onions with a pinch of salt. Cook 10 to 12 minutes until soft and gold at the edges. Don't rush the browning.
2. Add the EBS blend and smoked paprika directly to the onions. Cook 30 seconds until fragrant. Blooming the spices in fat is the real gain over steeping them in the liquid.
3. Add the beans, the full box of bone broth, water to 3 L total, bay leaves, and harissa.
4. Hard boil 10 minutes. Skim the foam.
5. Drop to the lowest flame that still gives a lazy bubble. Lid cracked. Simmer 3 to 4 hours. Check water hourly and top up to keep the beans submerged by an inch. Stir gently each hour.
6. Add the artichoke hearts from frozen for the last 45 to 60 minutes. Any earlier and they go to mush and turn metallic.
7. Doneness: a bean smears flat between your fingers with no chalk in the center. Pull the bay leaves.
8. Off heat, squeeze in the lemon. Artichokes want the acid, and it lifts the whole pot after that many hours.
9. Cool uncovered 45 minutes, then refrigerate overnight in the liquid. The beans firm up and the broth thickens.

## Notes
- This is a fiber and carb vehicle more than a protein driver. A 250 g portion gives 12 g fiber for 227 calories, so pair it with a real protein on the side.
- Don't cook meat into the pot. It welds the beans and the protein together and kills the ability to portion them independently. Cook the protein separately and portion it on.
- Pot choice matters. A 7 L pot is too wide: thin bean bed, heavy evaporation, uneven heat. A 4 to 5 quart pot is correct, with a deeper liquid column and less babysitting.
- Gigante is the right bean for a multi-hour braise. Chickpeas hold too firm, and lupini are pre-cooked.
- No soak needed; the long stove time does the work.
- **Slow cooker variant:** do the sauté and the 10-minute boil and skim on the stove, then transfer. Cook on low 6 to 8 hours, checking from 6. Drop the liquid to ~2.5 L since slow cookers barely evaporate. Lid on, no stirring.
- Never leave a gas burner going in an empty house. Killing the flame and picking it back up later costs nothing; total simmer time is what matters, not continuity.
