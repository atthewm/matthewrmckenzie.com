Version 3, revised after the v2 bake came out cakey. Cocoa powder, honey, and browned butter in a 9x13 pan at 300°F, mixed just enough to combine. Grams are primary throughout; volume equivalents are for convenience only, so weigh everything.

## Ingredients
- 180 g granulated sugar (scant 1 cup)
- 170 g honey (½ cup)
- 85 g Dutch-process cocoa powder (1 cup)
- 85 g unsalted butter (6 tbsp), browned down to ~70 g
- 72 g avocado oil (⅓ cup)
- 200 g eggs, 4 large, shelled weight
- 4.5 g salt (¾ tsp)
- 8 g vanilla (2 tsp)
- 125 g all-purpose flour (1 cup)
- No baking soda

## Instructions
1. Oven to 300°F. Parchment in the 9x13, overhang on the long sides.
2. Brown the 85 g butter over medium in a light-colored pan. It foams, quiets down, then the solids go amber and smell nutty. Pull it right there; amber to burnt is about 20 seconds. Scrape every solid in. About 70 g left.
3. Whisk the 72 g oil into the hot butter, then the 85 g cocoa until glossy. Set aside to cool to barely warm. It has to actually cool or it scrambles the eggs.
4. Beat the 200 g eggs with the salt on low, 30 seconds, just until broken up and uniform. No pale, no thick, no ribbon stage.
5. Add the 180 g sugar and 170 g honey. Beat on low another 30 seconds, just until combined. Should look wet and loose, not creamy.
6. Pour in the cooled cocoa mixture and vanilla. Mix on low 15 seconds or fold by hand.
7. Add the 125 g flour. Mix on lowest speed or fold until no dry streaks, then stop.
8. Bake 24 to 28 minutes. Center slightly underset, toothpick with damp crumbs. Lift the slab out by the parchment immediately.
9. Cool 90 minutes before cutting.

## Notes

### What changed from v2
v2 baked at 23+ minutes came out well-baked but cakey, with a lighter open band on top and a denser dark band below: classic aerated-batter separation. The bake was fine; the mixing was the problem.
- **Aeration was the cause, not the flour.** v2 called for beating eggs 2 minutes then another 3 minutes with sugar, which built a foam that set into a sponge. The original card's 1 cup flour has always produced fudgy, so flour stays at 125 g.
- **Mixing cut to 30 seconds on low at each stage.** Combine only: no whipping, no ribbon. A hand mixer is fine, just not at speed.
- **Baking soda removed.** It was added for honey browning, but it is also leavening and fudgy brownies want none.
- **Sugar 200 g to 180 g.**
- **Bake time up to 24 to 28 minutes** to match the denser, unaerated batter.

Expect a shorter, flatter slab with no aeration. Reduced height is not underbaking.

### Carried over from v1 and v2
- **Cocoa powder replaces the bars.** 4 oz unsweetened chocolate carries only ~57 g cocoa solids; 85 g cocoa is pure solids, so about 1.5x the chocolate intensity. Tradeoff: cocoa powder has no cocoa butter, which is part of what makes brownies dense at room temp.
- **Fat at ~142 g total** to replace the cocoa butter lost with the bars. Browned butter plus avocado oil; the oil keeps them soft on day three.
- **Half the sugar swapped for honey.** Moisture and flavor, not a macro win. Honey is roughly the same glucose/fructose split sucrose breaks into.
- **300°F instead of 325°F.** Fructose browns well below sucrose.
- **Salt at ¾ tsp.** ¼ tsp was flat at this cocoa load.
- **Espresso powder dropped.** Browned butter supplies the roasted aromatics instead.

### If it still is not dense enough
- Next lever is one extra egg yolk. Flour stays at 125 g.
- For a flour change, swap a third of the all-purpose for oat flour rather than going all-in.
