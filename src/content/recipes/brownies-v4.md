Version 4 of the cocoa and browned-butter brownie: less cane sugar, half rye flour, and hand-whisked in one bowl for a denser, fudgier slab. Grams are primary throughout; volume equivalents are for convenience only, so weigh everything.

**Pan:** 9x13, parchment with overhang on the long sides. **Oven:** 300°F. **Bake:** 22 to 26 minutes.

## Ingredients
- 150 g granulated sugar (¾ cup)
- 170 g honey (½ cup)
- 85 g Dutch-process cocoa powder (1 cup)
- 85 g unsalted butter (6 tbsp), browned, yields ~70 g
- 72 g avocado oil (⅓ cup)
- 200 g eggs, 4 large, shelled weight
- 4.5 g fine salt (¾ tsp)
- 8 g vanilla extract (2 tsp)
- 60 g rye flour (½ cup), light or medium
- 65 g all-purpose flour (½ cup)
- ~15 g powdered sugar for dusting, after cooling
- No baking soda, no baking powder

## Instructions
1. Oven to 300°F. Line the 9x13.
2. Brown the 85 g butter in a light-colored pan over medium heat. It foams, quiets, then the milk solids go amber and smell nutty. Pull at amber; amber to burnt is about 20 seconds. Scrape every brown solid in.
3. Whisk the 72 g avocado oil into the hot butter. Whisk in the 85 g cocoa until glossy and smooth. Cool until barely warm to the touch.
4. Whisk the 150 g sugar and 170 g honey into the cocoa mixture until the sugar feels mostly dissolved, about 30 seconds. This is where the shiny top comes from.
5. Whisk in the 200 g eggs one at a time, then the 4.5 g salt and 8 g vanilla. Stop as soon as each is incorporated. Hand whisk only, no mixer.
6. Fold in the 60 g rye and 65 g all-purpose until no dry streaks, then stop.
7. Spread into the pan. Bake 22 to 26 minutes. Center slightly underset, toothpick with damp crumbs.
8. Lift out by the parchment immediately. Cool on a rack 90 minutes. Do not rest the slab on a solid board; it traps steam and softens the bottom.
9. Dust with powdered sugar only after fully cool, or it melts in and disappears.
10. Cut 6 x 4 for 24 pieces (5 cuts across the long side, 3 across the short side). Long knife, one pull, wipe between cuts.

## Notes

### Changes from v3, and why
- **Cane sugar 180 g to 150 g.** About 17% less. Honey stays at 170 g and carries the humectant job, so this is safe. Expect a slightly duller top and a slightly shorter soft shelf life. Below ~130 g cane the texture goes cakey, so do not push further without adjusting honey. Powdered sugar on top compensates for perceived sweetness.
- **Rye at about half the flour.** 60 g rye + 65 g all-purpose, total 125 g unchanged. Rye deepens chocolate flavor and reads nutty, not like rye bread. Lower gluten pushes fudgier.
- **Mixer removed, order reversed.** v3 beat eggs, then eggs plus sugar, which was still more aeration than intended. v4 dissolves sugar and honey into the warm fat first, then eggs one at a time by hand. Denser, fudgier, and dissolving sugar in warm fat recovers some of the shiny top lost to the sugar cut.
- **Bake window pulled 2 minutes** to account for less sugar holding moisture.

### Use Dutch-process cocoa only
The first batch read slightly more bitter than v3 because it used mostly natural (non-alkalized) cocoa. Natural cocoa is more acidic and harsher, and this recipe has no baking soda to offset it. Use Dutch-process (alkalized) cocoa only.

### Flavor-only options, not in the base
- Flaky salt on top before baking. Largest single lift in perceived chocolate intensity.
- 2 to 3 g espresso powder into the cocoa mixture at step 3.
- 40 g unsweetened chocolate melted in at step 3. Adds cocoa butter, cleaner edge snap, deeper flavor.
- 20 to 30 g tahini at step 3, only if a nutty sesame edge is wanted.
- 30 to 40 g cacao nibs folded in at step 6.
